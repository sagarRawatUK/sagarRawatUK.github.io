The problem with breeding groups and classifieds

Most owners looking for a mate for their pet start in the same places: Facebook groups, WhatsApp groups and classified sites. They're free and busy, and sooner or later someone replies. But nothing in a post or a comment thread is designed to answer the questions that matter most:

  • Is this pairing safe? Nobody checks whether two animals are related, too young or too old, or both carriers of the same inherited condition.
  • Are the health claims real? "Fully vaccinated, all clear" in a caption is hard to verify, and the details you need are scattered across photos and messages.
  • Is this a good match, or just a nearby one? Posts get sorted by recency, not compatibility.
  • Is the other owner genuine? Pet listings are a well-known target for scams, and an open group has little to filter them out.

What Breedly does differently

A compatibility score you can read

Every match comes with a Pet Compatibility Score. Instead of a black-box number, the score is built from clear factors: breed and genetics, temperament, health, pedigree and inbreeding, age and readiness, and distance. You can see why a pairing is strong, weak or unsuitable, and make the call with the right context.

Health-aware matching

Breedly factors in carrier status, health screening, pedigree, age suitability and recovery windows between litters. Health details live on the pet's profile, so they can be shared and reviewed properly rather than buried in a chat.

Unsafe pairings are blocked, not just scored low

Some pairings shouldn't happen at all: close relatives, animals that are too young or too old, or two carriers of the same recessive condition. Breedly blocks these outright, so a harmful match is never casually suggested.

Structured requests, not scattered DMs

Send and receive breeding requests with the compatibility score attached. Received, sent and matched requests each have their own list, and the compatibility snapshot stays with each conversation so you can always see what you agreed to.

Complete pet profiles

Keep health details, pedigree, history and breeding records in one structured profile. It's easier for you to manage and easier for the other owner to trust.

When groups are still useful

  • Reach. Big groups have thousands of members today. Breedly is in early access, so there may be fewer matches in your area while the network grows.
  • Rare breeds. Breed-specific groups can be the best place to find experienced owners and advice for less common breeds.
  • Community knowledge. Groups are good for questions, recommendations and local vets. Breedly is a matching tool, not a forum.

Whichever route you use, get health testing and breeding advice from a qualified vet, and follow your local rules on breeding and registration.
