iReject vs Mend: a private no-contact app for the hardest moments

Mend helps you understand heartbreak through guided audio. iReject is for the moment your thumb is hovering over their name. Here's how they compare.

By Sagar Rawat26 Oct 20265 min read

The short answer

Choose Mend if you want to learn and listen: a large library of guided audio trainings and a community of people going through the same thing. Choose iReject if you need help staying no contact day to day: a counter, an SOS to open instead of texting your ex, a private journal, and no account.

Why people look for a Mend alternative

Mend is one of the best-known names in breakup recovery. It began as an app built around short daily audio trainings, and today its signature program offers hundreds of audio trainings, journal prompts, a 60-day ex detox module and a members' community. If you want to understand why heartbreak hurts the way it does, that's a lot of good material.

But the hardest part of a breakup often isn't a lack of understanding. It's 1am, you've had a bad day, and you're about to send "hey, how are you?" In that moment, a course isn't what stops you. People looking for an alternative usually want:

  • Something for the urge itself. A thing to open instead of the chat.
  • A visible no-contact count. A number that makes the cost of breaking it feel real.
  • Privacy. No sign-up, no community, and a journal that never leaves the phone.
  • Something light. Two minutes a day, not another course to keep up with.

What iReject does differently

A counter that makes no contact visible

iReject's home screen shows how long you've gone without contact, down to the minute, with milestones at 7, 30, 60 and 90 days. You can set the date it started, so the count reflects your real progress from day one. It's there to keep momentum visible, not to turn healing into a performance.

An SOS for when you want to text them

When the urge hits, open iReject first. The SOS puts your own reasons for leaving in front of you, in your own words, at exactly the moment you're romanticising the past. If you need to say something, write it as an unsent message and keep it private, instead of sending it in the middle of a spiral. And if you need to talk it through, the AI listener gives a reflective, non-judgmental response.

Tools to calm your body first

Urges are physical as much as mental. The Support tab has guided breathing exercises such as 4-7-8 and box breathing, plus short, practical coping strategies for panic and urges.

Remember why you left

After a breakup, memory edits itself into something kinder than it was. iReject gives you a place to write down why the relationship wasn't right, whether it was toxic, a situationship or simply the wrong person. The anchors wall keeps screenshots and notes of what really happened, archived rather than deleted, so you can check the facts without reliving them.

A journal, a journey and your progress

The private journal includes prompts for days you don't know where to start. A 10-week guided journey adds small tasks and milestones. You can log moods, slip-ups and wins, see how you're doing over time, and set gentle daily reminders.

Private by design

No account is needed. Your journal stays on your device, and you can lock the app with a passcode. iReject has no ads.

When Mend is the better choice

  • You want to understand what you're going through. Mend's library of guided audio goes much deeper into the psychology of heartbreak than iReject does.
  • You'd rather listen than write. If journaling isn't for you, a daily audio session may suit you better.
  • You want company. Mend's community connects you with people who get it. iReject is deliberately private.

They also work well together: Mend to learn and process, iReject in your pocket for the moments in between.

Neither app is therapy. If you're struggling to cope, or having thoughts of harming yourself, please reach out to a mental health professional or a local crisis line right away.

How to start with iReject

  1. Install iReject from Google Play. There's no sign-up.
  2. Set the date your no contact started.
  3. Write at least three reasons it wasn't right. These are what you'll read in the SOS.
  4. Set a passcode and a daily reminder.
  5. Next time you feel the urge, open iReject before you open their chat.
